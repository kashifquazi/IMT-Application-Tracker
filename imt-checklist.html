Publications
Best single option — PubMed citation required for most
0/8 pts ▾
Select the highest applicable option. All publications must relate to medicine. Work that is in press (accepted but not yet published) is eligible for the top two options only. You must be able to provide a PubMed ID (PMID) as evidence for any PubMed-cited entry.
First author, joint-first author, or corresponding author of original research
Published in a peer-reviewed journal with a unique PubMed ID (PMID), or formally accepted "in press". Original research only — not reviews, case reports, or letters.
+8 pts
Co-author on original research
Named on the PubMed author list for published or in-press original research. You are listed as an author (not acknowledgements only).
+6 pts
Multiple other PubMed-cited publications
Editorials, systematic reviews, case reports, letters — more than one article. All must have unique PMIDs.
+5 pts
Book chapter(s) related to medicine
Published by an independent publishing house — self-published works do not count. Must relate to medicine or healthcare.
+5 pts
Single other PubMed-cited publication
One editorial, review, case report, or letter — with a PMID. Case reports in BMJ Case Reports, JRSM, or specialty journals qualify.
+3 pts
Abstracts, non-peer-reviewed articles, or non-PubMed-cited publications
Conference abstracts, articles in non-indexed journals, or publications without a PMID.

None of the above

Teaching Experience
Best single option — formal feedback required
0/5 pts ▾
Select the highest applicable option. All options require formal documented feedback from learners. Informal or undocumented teaching does not score. Evidence such as a letter or certificate of recognition from your organisation is required.
Organised a formal teaching programme (≥3 months, regular sessions, formal feedback)
You took the lead in organising the programme — e.g. worked with local tutors to set up a teaching series. Must have run for approximately three months or more with documented formal feedback from attendees.
+5 pts
Regular teaching as part of a defined programme (≥3 months, formal feedback)
Includes bedside/classroom teaching, mentoring, or tutoring as part of an established programme. Approximately three months or more. Formal feedback required.
+3 pts
Occasional teaching (minimum 3 sessions, formal feedback)
Less than three months in total, but at least three teaching sessions with formal feedback collected after each.

University-accredited programme of at least 60 academic credit points. Confirms formal education theory underpinning your teaching practice.

Must include at least six hours of synchronous (live, interactive) training time. Workshops, faculty development days, or structured online courses with live sessions can qualify.

Quality Improvement
Best single option — PDSA methodology
0/4 pts ▾
Select the highest applicable option. Quality Improvement Projects (QIPs) use PDSA cycles (Plan–Do–Study–Act) or equivalent methodology. Direct, documented involvement in each stage is required — passive data collection alone is insufficient for full marks.
All stages of two complete QI cycles
Involved in every stage of two full PDSA or equivalent cycles. Both cycles must be documented with a measurable baseline, intervention, and outcome.
+4 pts
Some stages of two cycles, OR all stages of one complete cycle
Either partial involvement across two cycles, or full involvement in a single complete PDSA cycle. Must include data collection and analysis.
+3 pts
Some stages of a single QI cycle
Partial involvement in one cycle — e.g. data collection only, or planning without completing implementation and review.

None of the above

MSRA Preparation
Does not score — but critical for shortlisting
Prep ▾
The MSRA (Multi-Specialty Recruitment Assessment) is sat before the portfolio assessment stage. Your MSRA score is a major shortlisting factor. It has two papers: Clinical Problem Solving (CPS) and Professional Dilemmas (PD).
Registered for MSRA and confirmed exam date
Book early — centres fill up fast. Check the IMT recruitment timeline each year for registration windows.
Reviewed the MSRA blueprint and syllabus topics
The MSRA blueprint is publicly available on the IMT recruitment microsite — understand what topics are tested.
Practising CPS questions regularly (300+ target)
Passmedicine, Pastest, and BMJ OnExamination all have CPS-style questions. Daily practice is strongly recommended.
Completing PD practice papers and ethical scenarios
PD tests ethical reasoning. Familiarise yourself with GMC Good Medical Practice principles. Avoid extreme answers.
Mock MSRA completed under timed conditions
Time pressure is the main challenge. Simulate exam conditions at least 2–3 weeks before your sitting date.
MRCP Preparation
Does not score in self-assessment — but relevant for IMT progression
Prep ▾
MRCP is not scored in the current self-assessment application form, but passing MRCP Part 1 before your IMT application makes you a stronger candidate and is required to progress within IMT. Starting early is highly recommended.
MRCP Part 1 revision plan created
Aim for a structured 4–6 month revision plan. Passmedicine, OnExamination, and PassPACES are key resources.
MRCP Part 1 sitting date identified (or passed)
Sittings are at Pearson VUE centres several times per year. Passing before your IMT application strengthens your overall profile.
MRCP Part 1 passed
A major milestone. MRCP Part 1 is required before you can sit MRCP Part 2 Written. Aim to pass before starting IMT ST1.
Courses & Certificates
Not directly scored — but required or portfolio-strengthening
Prep ▾
ALS is a mandatory requirement for IMT posts. Additional courses strengthen your application narrative even where not directly scored.
ALS (Advanced Life Support) — completed and in date
Mandatory for IMT. Valid for 4 years. Book via Resuscitation Council UK well in advance.
Good Clinical Practice (GCP) certification obtained
Required for any research involvement. Free via NIHR Learn. Valid for 3 years — renew before application if needed.
Specialty course completed (echo, endoscopy, cardiology, etc.)
Demonstrates subspecialty interest relevant to IMT. BSE level 1 echo, basic endoscopy, or similar courses are well regarded.
Application & Portfolio Admin
Essential tasks — do not leave until the last minute
Admin ▾
Administrative tasks are often left too late. The Oriel portal opens and closes to tight deadlines. Having your evidence ready in advance is critical — a randomised audit may require you to submit supporting documents.
Full GMC registration confirmed (full registration now required)
Full GMC registration is now required at time of application. A licence to practise is not required, but full registration must be confirmed on the register.
Registered on Oriel (oriel.nhs.uk) and profile complete
Set up your account before the application window opens. Delays at this stage can cause you to miss the deadline.
Current IMT person specification read and reviewed in full
Available on imtrecruitment.org.uk. Scoring criteria change annually — always check the current year's specification.
Evidence portfolio compiled — publications, certs, audit docs
Collect supporting evidence in a single folder. You may be selected for a randomised audit and required to submit this evidence at short notice.
ORCID iD created and publications linked
Free at orcid.org. Essential for verifying your publication record. Set this up before completing your application.
Personal statement drafted and reviewed by a senior colleague
Focus on commitment to internal medicine, career goals, and key portfolio achievements. Get this reviewed by an IMT doctor.
Two referees approached, confirmed, and briefed
Ideally a consultant who knows your clinical work well. Give them your CV and personal statement in advance to help them write a strong reference.
Preferred deaneries / regions shortlisted
Consider training opportunities, subspecialty availability, and personal circumstances. IMT is a two-year programme initially.
